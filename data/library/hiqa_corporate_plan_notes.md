# Plain-English notes on HIQA Corporate Plan 2025-2027

Summarised passage by passage by Deeksha Gulati from the public document: https://www.hiqa.ie/sites/default/files/2025-05/HIQA-Corporate-Plan-25-27.pdf

## About HIQA (p.2)

HIQA is an independent statutory body that promotes safety and quality in health and social care. Its functions include setting standards, regulating social care and health services, monitoring services, health technology assessment, advising on health information, and running the National Care Experience Programme of service-user surveys with the Department of Health and the HSE.

## Foreword (p.4)

The plan runs to 2027, which marks HIQA's 20th anniversary. HIQA ran an extensive engagement programme with patients, people using services, policymakers, providers, advocacy groups, staff and the public, asking what had worked, what challenges lay ahead and what the priorities should be.

## Foreword (p.4)

HIQA moved away from traditional goal setting towards tangible outcomes it wants to achieve by 2027. These outcomes are described as the benchmarks for measuring the plan's success, alongside longer-term outcomes beyond the three years.

## Context (p.5)

New statutory functions are adding to HIQA's remit. The Patient Safety Act 2023 makes reporting of notifiable incidents mandatory and extends oversight to private healthcare providers. The Health (Miscellaneous Provisions) (No. 2) Act 2024 lets the Chief Inspector issue compliance notices and allows collection and publication of operational data from nursing homes.

## Context (p.5)

Other developments include monitoring permanent International Protection Accommodation Services centres, joint clinical assessments under the EU Health Technology Assessment Regulation from 2025, the European Health Data Space Regulation, critical entities resilience rules, the Human Tissue Act 2024 and monitoring quality across the new Sláintecare health regions.

## Context (p.6)

The plan notes that advances in artificial intelligence are likely to have an increasing impact on healthcare. It also notes that HIQA hosts the National Immunisation Advisory Committee from March 2025, must meet public sector climate targets for 2030, align with sustainability reporting rules, and sees UN Sustainable Development Goal 3 on health and wellbeing as especially relevant.

## Context (p.7)

Bills under consideration that could affect HIQA include cybersecurity rules under NIS-2, the Health Information Bill 2024, licensing of professional home support providers, and licensing of hospitals and high-risk healthcare activities.

## Context (p.7)

HIQA ran a scenario planning exercise while developing the plan. It looked at long-term macro factors, tactical issues for delivering the 2025–2027 outcomes, and internal capabilities, and concluded that HIQA faces moderate to extensive change.

## Vision and mission (p.8)

The vision is health and social care services that consistently deliver excellent standards of care and the best possible outcomes for the people of Ireland. The mission is to enable the best possible outcomes for all through evidence-informed decision-making, standard setting and regulation.

## Values (p.10)

HIQA's seven values are human rights, being people centred, being fair, objective and equitable, being open and accountable, excellence, quality, and collaboration.

## Long-term outcomes (p.13)

Four long-term outcomes sit behind the vision: safe, joined-up care across all services (service focus); evidence-informed decisions that improve outcomes (evidence-informed impact); a high-performing organisation (governance and organisational effectiveness); and a positive working environment (people).

## Outcomes 2025–2027 (p.14)

Six outcomes are set for 2025–2027: regulation and standards enabling better outcomes; influencing providers to improve service user experiences; providing evidence for policy; delivering value through agile ways of working and good governance; being sustainable and inclusive; and being an employer of choice.

## Outcomes 2025–2027 (p.15)

Under the service user outcome, a key priority is strengthening the use of data analytics, intelligence and real-time information to support regulatory effectiveness. HIQA also commits to keep evaluating its own regulatory and monitoring processes.

## Resources (p.16)

HIQA's main funder is the Department of Health, with registration fees and earmarked funding from other bodies. The plan assumes remit expansion happens in phases with extra resources, timely approval of staff requests, and funding that keeps pace with pay and operating costs.

## Activities (p.17)

Because HIQA is moving to an outcomes focus, it says it must review how it measures success. Developing an outcome framework and a mechanism to monitor progress is named as a key priority for the first year of the plan.

## Activities: service focus (p.17)

Actions include publishing reports on findings across healthcare, radiation, IPAS centres, older person, disability and children's services; extending the Chief Inspector's remit to home support; statutory investigations; updating national standards; risk-based regulation; and annual reporting on open disclosure.

## Activities: service users (p.18)

Actions include extending the National Care Experience Programme to cancer and mental health services and to hard-to-reach groups, publishing annual overview reports, reviewing regulatory effectiveness after developments in nursing homes, and using data and analytics to share learning across sectors.

## Activities: evidence (p.19)

Actions include health technology assessments and evidence syntheses, a strategic research function, work on a National Data Access Body, developing an ethical and safe use guidance framework for AI in health and social care, and supporting an Evidence Synthesis Hub for future health crises.

## Activities: governance (p.20)

Actions include reviewing the Target Operating Model, including assessing how AI and data analytics could improve operational performance; evaluating HIQA's impact with specific impact measures; a Digital and Data Strategy; and a Quality, Risk and Compliance Strategy for 2026–2028.

## Activities: sustainability and people (p.20)

Actions include implementing the Climate Action Plan, diversity, equality and inclusion plans, sustainability reporting, a People and Culture Strategy, strategic workforce plans, a Learning and Development Strategy, and an employee engagement plan.

## Implementation and monitoring (p.22)

The Board and Executive Management Team will monitor the plan, alongside a performance delivery agreement with the Department of Health. HIQA says it needs to further develop a methodology to evaluate its impact and measure the value it adds to Irish society.

## Appendix: human rights and equality (p.23)

Under its public sector equality and human rights duty, HIQA commits to prioritise measuring the real-world impact of regulation rather than only tracking outputs, and to keep gathering qualitative feedback from people using services.
